Notes: The DAMA-DMBOK Data Management Framework provides a functional structure for implementing enterprise data management practices, establishing a common vocabulary, and serving as the definitive reference for the Certified Data Management Professional (CDMP) certification. It defines Data Management as the development and execution of plans, policies, and practices that deliver and protect the value of data assets throughout their lifecycles. The second edition (DMBOK2) reformulates the framework to account for developments in Data Governance, Big Data, Data Science, and Data Ethics.
The sources describe the framework through several integrated models and concepts:

1. Visual Representations of the Framework
The framework utilizes three primary visuals to describe the scope and detail of the profession:
• The DAMA Wheel: This model defines the eleven Knowledge Areas (KAs) of data management, placing Data Governance at the center to ensure consistency and balance among the other ten functions positioned around the perimeter.
• The Environmental Factors Hexagon: This diagram illustrates the relationship between people, process, and technology. It places Goals and Principles at the center, surrounded by components like Activities, Tools, Techniques, and Roles and Responsibilities, which provide a key for reading Knowledge Area details.
• Knowledge Area Context Diagrams: Based on the SIPOC model (Suppliers, Inputs, Processes, Outputs, Consumers), these diagrams put Activities at the center, driven by business and technical drivers to produce specific deliverables for stakeholders.

2. Knowledge Areas (KAs)
The DMBOK is structured around eleven core disciplines that intersect with one another and other organizational functions:
• Data Governance: Oversight and decision rights over data.
• Data Architecture: Blueprints for aligning data assets with organizational strategy.
• Data Modeling and Design: Discovery and communication of data requirements.
• Data Storage and Operations: Implementation and support of stored data.
• Data Security: Maintaining privacy and preventing breaches.
• Data Integration and Interoperability: Movement and consolidation of data.
• Document and Content Management: Managing unstructured data lifecycles.
• Reference and Master Data: Maintaining a "version of truth" for shared entities.
• Data Warehousing and Business Intelligence: Managing data for analysis and reporting.
• Metadata Management: Enabling access to definitions and data flows.
• Data Quality: Measuring and improving the fitness of data for use.

3. Functional Dependencies and Lifecycle Models
The framework recognizes that Knowledge Areas are not interchangeable but have specific interdependencies described by various models:
• Aiken’s Pyramid: This model outlines a logical progression, suggesting that organizations must build a foundation in Phase 1 (Modeling, Storage, Security) before they can successfully reach Phase 4 (Advanced Practices like Data Science).
• DAMA Functional Area Dependencies (Geuens’ Model): This model recognizes that Business Intelligence and Analytics depend on a foundation of Governance, Metadata, and Security, as well as the systems and applications that feed them.
• DAMA Data Management Function Framework: This maps Knowledge Areas to the Data Lifecycle, categorizing them as Plan & Design (Architecture, Modeling), Enable & Maintain (Storage, MDM, Integration), or Use & Enhance (BI, Data Science).

4. Foundational Principles
The framework is built upon twelve data management principles, including the recognition that Data is an asset with unique properties, its value should be expressed in economic terms, and that managing data effectively requires Metadata, planning, cross-functional skills, and leadership commitment. Furthermore, it emphasizes that Data Management is lifecycle management and that requirements for data must drive Information Technology decisions, rather than the reverse.
