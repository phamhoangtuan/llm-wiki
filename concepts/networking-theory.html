# Networking Theory

The strategic practice of building professional relationships, anchored by the Theory of Weak Ties — the idea that acquaintances and strangers are more likely to bridge you to new opportunities than close friends.

Theory of Weak Ties

From sociologist Mark Granovetter's landmark 1973 study:

  • Strong ties: Close friends and family — know the same people and information you do
  • Weak ties: Acquaintances, former colleagues, friends-of-friends — access different networks
  • Finding: Most job opportunities come through weak ties, not strong ones
"Your close friends know the same people you do. It's the acquaintance who knows someone you don't."

Tiered Outreach Model (#ENTRYLEVELBOSS)

  1. Board of Directors: Personal network — former colleagues, mentors, friends. Start here for practice and warm introductions.
  2. Target Companies: Direct outreach to people at specific desired employers. Research first, personalize.
  3. Magical Sparks: Strangers whose careers or work inspire curiosity. Pure weak ties — the highest-risk, highest-reward category.

The "Stupid-Easy" Principle

The core tactical principle: remove all friction for the person being asked:

  • Provide specific time slots (not "whenever works for you")
  • Offer to travel to their location
  • Make the ask small and clear ("15-minute chat about your transition to X")
  • Never ask for a job — ask for information, advice, or perspective

Networking vs "Networking"

Asking for jobs Seeking insight
