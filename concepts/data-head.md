## Summary

A **Data Head** is a professional — manager, executive, or domain expert — who can think critically about data, statistics, and machine learning without necessarily being a technical practitioner. The term describes a mindset, not a job title: someone who moves from passive consumer of data claims to active, informed participant in data-driven decisions (source: [[sources/becoming-data-head]]).

## The Four Competencies

| Competency | What It Means |
| ------------ | --------------- |
| **Think** | Adopt a [[statistical-thinking | statistical lens]]: expect variation, be skeptical, define problems before analyzing |
| **Speak** | Challenge statistics: "compared to what?" — demand context, sample sizes, null hypotheses |
| **Understand** | Grasp what models do without coding: supervised vs unsupervised, regression vs classification, overfitting vs underfitting |
| **Ensure** | Manage human and technical risks: navigate data personalities, avoid data leakage, drive continuous learning |

## What a Data Head Is NOT

A Data Head is not a "unicorn" with a PhD. The role is about **critical engagement** with data work, not the ability to write Python or train neural networks. A Data Head can:

- Ask whether a dataset is representative
- Question whether an effect size is practically significant
- Recognize when a model is being overhyped
- Bridge the communication gap between technical teams and business stakeholders

## Data Personalities

Successful Data Heads understand and navigate different orientations toward data:

| Personality | Behavior | How to Engage |
| ------------- | ---------- | --------------- |
| **Enthusiast** | Believes every AI claim uncritically | Ground with concrete success criteria, failure modes |
| **Cynic** | Rejects all data as manipulated | Build trust with transparent methodology, small wins |
| **Indifferent** | Ignores data entirely | Connect data to their personal goals and metrics |
