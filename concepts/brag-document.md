## Summary

A **Brag Document** is a continuously maintained record of your achievements, metrics, and feedback — framed in terms of business outcomes rather than activity. Addy Osmani's *The Effective Software Engineer* lists it as the first of four implicit rules for promotion, precisely because promotion rests on **demonstrated scope and outcome impact**, not tenure (source: [[sources/the-effective-software-engineer-osmani]]).

## Why Keep One

- **Memory fails** — by review season you will have forgotten the quarter's wins; a running log preserves the evidence
- **Promotion is demonstrated, not granted** — you must already be working at the next level's scope, and the document is how that is shown
- **Calibration is competitive** — managers argue your case in rooms you are not in; concrete artifacts travel further than impressions
- **It protects against recounting only the visible** — quiet wins (mentoring, debt repayment, risk averted) are exactly what goes unremembered

## What to Record

| Record | Weak Version ❌ | Strong Version ✅ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Outcomes** | "Refactored the payment module" | "Cut payment retries 30%, reducing support load" |
| **Metrics** | "Improved latency" | "Cut p95 latency 40%; conversion up 3%" |
| **Scope** | "Worked on the migration" | "Led 3-team migration of 12 services, no downtime" |
| **Feedback** | (nothing) | Quote the PM who said launch risk dropped |
| **Invisible work** | (nothing) | Mentored 2 juniors to independent delivery |

The framing rule: **outcome over output** (see [[output-vs-outcome]]) — business results, not activity.

## The Four Implicit Promotion Rules (Osmani)

1. **Maintain a "brag document"** — running record of achievements, metrics, feedback, framed by business results
2. **Build your personal brand** — be known for consistent excellence (debugging wizard, clean-API architect, dedicated mentor)
3. **Cultivate sponsors** — senior leaders willing to advocate for you in calibration meetings
4. **Choose your track** — Individual Contributor (Staff+) vs Engineering Management (EM)

## Relationship to Leveling

The document is also a **gap analysis** tool: compare what you have recorded against the next level's expectations.

| Level | Focus | What the document should show |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mid-Level (L4)** | Execution & delivery | Independent delivery on clear scope, maintainable code, thoughtful reviews |
| **Senior (L5)** | Project ownership & team elevation | Ambiguous goals turned into architecture, coordinated execution, juniors mentored |
| **Staff (L6+)** | Organizational strategy | Cross-team architectural risk identified, technical strategy aligned to business |
