## Summary

**Coaching** is the practice of developing people by asking questions instead of giving answers — staying curious so the other person owns both the problem and the solution. Michael Bungay Stanier's *The Coaching Habit* is the canonical toolkit, built on a small set of repeated **"What" questions** (source: [[sources/the-coaching-habit-stanier]]).

## The Essential Questions

| Question | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| "What's on your mind?" | Opens the conversation to what matters most |
| "And what else?" | Unlocks deeper insights without jumping to advice |
| "What's the real challenge here for you?" | Shifts focus from surface issues to the core problem |
| "What do you want from me?" | Clarifies expectations, avoids assumptions |
| "What was most useful here for you?" | Encapsulates learning and reinforces value |

## Principles

- **Stick to "What" questions** — "What" keeps the conversation open; "Why" triggers defensiveness
- **Stay curious** — the stance of not knowing beats the stance of fixing
- **Say less, ask more** — advice creates dependency; questions build self-sufficiency
- **One small habit at a time** — behavior change sticks when the habit is minimal and repeatable

## Where Coaching Lives

- **1:1 meetings**: "What's on your mind?" opens the employee's agenda; "What do you want from me?" prevents the manager from guessing
- **Feedback**: questions elicit self-discovery instead of imposed correction
- **Mentoring**: the mentor coaches the juniors to find answers, then shares experience
