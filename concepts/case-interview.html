Concept

Case Interview

Source: the-1-percent-case-interviewUpdated: 2026-06-15
interviewconsultingcase-interviewproblem-solvingcareer

Summary

The consulting case interview is a structured problem-solving conversation that simulates a real client engagement. Unlike technical interviews that test coding ability, case interviews test analytical thinking, structured communication, and business judgment. Only ~1% of applicants receive offers from top firms (McKinsey, BCG, Bain) — the difference is not knowledge but how you think, structure, and communicate.

First Principles > Frameworks

The old approach — memorize 50 frameworks and apply the "right" one — no longer works. Modern firms test whether you can deconstruct novel problems from scratch:

Old Way New Way (The 1%)
Apply Profitability Framework to profit problems MECE deconstruction: Revenue ↓? Cost ↑? Drill into each
Apply Market Entry Framework to market questions First Principles: What makes this market attractive? What are our capabilities?
Memorize 3Cs, 4Ps, Porter's Five Forces Use frameworks as vocabulary, not crutches
MECE: Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive — branches don't overlap, and together they cover the entire problem space.

Core Methodologies

Pyramid Principle: Communicate Top-Down

❌ Traditional storytelling: "The context was... we analyzed... discovered... so we recommend A"
✅ Pyramid Principle: "We recommend A. Three reasons: 1) ..., 2) ..., 3) ..."

Start with the answer, then provide supporting arguments. This is how executives communicate — and how interviewers expect you to communicate.

SCORE Framework for Fit Interviews

Component Purpose
S - Situation Set the context
C - Complication The challenge that arose
O - Outcome expectation What success looked like
R - Remedial action What you specifically did
E - End result Quantifiable impact
Fit interviews matter as much as case interviews. The "airport test": would the interviewer want to be stuck with you for 3 hours?

9-Step Math Approach

  1. Listen carefully to the question
  2. Clarify what exactly needs calculation
  3. Structure the approach
  4. Round numbers intelligently (1.98M → 2M)
  5. Calculate step-by-step, verbalizing
  6. Sanity check
  7. Interpret in case context
  8. State answer clearly
  9. Recommend next steps
Directionally correct matters more than perfectly precise. Show you can reason with numbers, not just compute them.

80/20 Preparation Strategy

Activity Time Allocation Rationale
Individual drills 80-90% Exposure to diverse problems, fast self-correction
Peer mock interviews 10-20% Practice real-time dynamics, but avoid echo chambers
Pro tip: Mock interview with mentors/coaches who have actual consulting experience — not just peers at your level.

McKinsey-Specific Formats

Format Style Preparation
Problem Solving Interview Interviewer-led — they guide, you follow Focus on logic, not driving the case
Personal Experience Interview (PEI) Deep dive into past experiences Use SCORE framework, prepare 5-7 stories
