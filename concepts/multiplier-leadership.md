## Summary

**Multiplier Leadership** is the operating model for leaders who scale themselves out of the critical path: instead of being the smartest person in the room, they make everyone else smarter. The measure of success is becoming **redundant** in daily operations — the team runs itself. Addy Osmani's *Leading Effective Engineering Teams* packages this as the **3E model** (Enable → Empower → Expand), the Multiplier vs Diminisher contrast, and the "Always Be Leaving" strategy (source: [[sources/leading-effective-engineering-teams-osmani]]).

## Multiplier vs Diminisher

| Multiplier (Nhân tử) | Diminisher (Người thu nhỏ) |
| --- | --- |
| Amplifies team intelligence | Hoards authority, makes every decision |
| Decodes jargon into simple terms | Uses jargon to impress/distance |
| Asks the right questions | Has all the answers |
| Builds a culture of interdependence | Creates dependence on the leader |

## The 3E Model

| Stage | Goal | Actions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ENABLE** | Define what "win" looks like | Set goals & metrics (e.g., adoption rate); share strategy clearly; **servant leadership** — absorb admin so engineers focus on value; let the team co-create **standards** (raised effectiveness ~23%) |
| **EMPOWER** | Remove obstacles | **Feed opportunities** (resource the most promising work); **starve problems** (resolve conflicts fast, remove bottlenecks); enable rather than impose |
| **EXPAND** | Build the "self-driving machine" | **Always Be Leaving** — coach until the team no longer needs you; raise the **bus factor** (no single point of failure); mentor new leaders, replicate success patterns |

## The "Always Be Leaving" Strategy

1. **Divide the problem space** — split projects into modular ownership
2. **Delegate to future leaders** — hand over areas, forcing members to own the outcome
3. **Adjust and iterate** — retreat to macro-management; intervene only when the machine malfunctions

> The test: if you go away for two weeks and the team panics, you're not a leader — you're a bottleneck.

## Bus Factor

The bus factor is the number of people whose departure would cripple the project. EXPAND's job is to raise it: no single point of failure, knowledge distributed, success patterns replicated. A successful leader is one who is "bus-factor friendly" — expendable in daily operations precisely because the team is empowered.
