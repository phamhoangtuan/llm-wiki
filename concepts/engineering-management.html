Concept

Engineering Management

Source: engineering-management-drasnerUpdated: 2026-08-15
engineering-managementleadershippeople-managementcareertrust

Summary

Engineering Management is the discipline of enabling the people around you to do their best work together. Unlike technical-leadership, which leads through influence without authority on the Staff+ path, engineering management leads through direct reports — and is often thrust upon engineers promoted for technical skill rather than managerial inclination. Sarah Drasner's Engineering Management for the Rest of Us is its pragmatic manual (source: sources/engineering-management-drasner).

The Four Sections

Section Focus
Your Team The human element: values, trust, vulnerability, inclusivity
Collaboration Communication as the core work: alignment with the why, meetings, feedback, change
Helping Your Team Do Their Best Work Prioritization (OKRs), scoping PRs, speed vs. debt
Your Work Self-care: decision fatigue, boundaries, resilience

Core Principles

  • Trust built in drops, lost in buckets — model vulnerability first to create psychological safety where teams learn from mistakes.
  • Alignment with the why — explain purpose behind tasks; engineers are motivated by clarity, not orders. See golden-circle.
  • Feedback as partnership — growth-oriented, not top-down critique. See feedback.
  • DRI and clear outcomes — good meetings have a Directly Responsible Individual.
  • Scope down PRs — small reviewable units reduce reviewer cognitive load and make code easier to test.
  • Speed ≠ rushing — bias toward action without accumulating massive technical debt.

Manager Self-Care

  • Decision fatigue — build systems for high-level and day-to-day prioritization to preserve energy for complex calls.
  • Boundaries — be interruption-driven so the team can be focus-driven, but protect yourself from burnout.
  • Resilience — a "manager cabal" or coaching processes the isolating parts of the role.
