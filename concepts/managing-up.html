Concept

Managing Up

Source: help-your-boss-help-you-kousenUpdated: 2026-10-11
careermanaging-upcommunicationtrustengineering-management

Summary

Managing Up is the deliberate practice of building a productive, trust-based relationship with your manager — treating the reporting line as a relationship to cultivate rather than a hierarchy to endure. Ken Kousen's Help Your Boss Help You is the canonical guide: cultivate trust through consistency, ownership, and tactful advocacy (source: sources/help-your-boss-help-you-kousen).

The Nine Practices

  1. Advise, don't decide — present options with risks/benefits; let your manager choose
  2. Prioritize timeliness — deliver "good enough" solutions quickly to build trust
  3. Own outcomes — proactively solve problems, ask for help when stuck, follow through
  4. Build loyalty first — support your manager publicly/privately to earn reciprocal trust
  5. Push back calmly — address harmful decisions respectfully, then refocus on solutions
  6. Use balanced pushback — cooperate fully, address issues immediately, then reset
  7. Keep it professional — avoid oversharing; assume oversights, not malice
  8. Own mistakes — apologize, fix errors, and share prevention plans
  9. Be indispensable — prioritize reliability, team alignment, and clear communication

Why It Matters

  • Trust is the currency — timeliness and reliability compound into autonomy and sponsorship
  • Pushback done right — calm, solution-focused disagreement protects the organization and the relationship
  • Ownership signals seniority — the employee who owns outcomes is the one who gets bigger outcomes

Managing Up vs. Being a Sycophant

Managing up is not flattery. The distinction: sycophants tell the boss what they want to hear; managing up means advising with options, pushing back tactfully on harmful decisions, and owning mistakes — advocacy anchored in honesty and results.
