## Summary

**Behavioral Habits** — from Marshall Goldsmith's *What Got You Here Won't Get You There* — are the interpersonal behaviors that either accelerate or derail a successful person's further rise. The book's thesis: the behaviors that got you here are often exactly what must change to get you *there*. The condensed guide is a nine-step curriculum from communication basics through accountability to emotional mastery (source: [[sources/what-got-you-here-goldsmith]]).

## The Nine Steps

1. **Share knowledge openly and listen actively** — build trust and collaboration
2. **Express gratitude for all ideas** — encourage open dialogue
3. **React neutrally to suggestions** — avoid immediate judgment
4. **Pause before responding; stay silent when angry** — maintain composure
5. **Ask "Why not?"** — inspire accountability and focus on future solutions
6. **Publicly acknowledge contributions and own mistakes** — without blame
7. **Replace criticism with curiosity** — foster innovation and safety
8. **Delay emotional reactions** — model leadership and self-control
9. **Apologize sincerely** — resolve conflicts and rebuild trust

## The Three-Stage Progression

| Stage | Steps | Theme |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Communication basics** | 1–3 | Open sharing, gratitude, neutrality |
| **Accountability** | 4–6 | Composure, "Why not?", acknowledgment & ownership |
| **Emotional mastery** | 7–9 | Curiosity, delayed reactions, sincere apology |

Lasting behavioral change requires all three layers — the social-skills surface alone is not enough.

## Why It Matters for Leaders

- Success makes people *less* receptive to feedback; these habits counter that drift
- Public acknowledgment and mistake-ownership build the trust that [[engineering-management]] relies on
- Curiosity instead of criticism is the concrete behavior behind [[coaching]]
