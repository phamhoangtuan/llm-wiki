# Feedback

**Feedback** in the context of skill acquisition is the information returned to a learner about their performance. In [[ultralearning|Ultralearning]], it is the sixth principle: the discipline of seeking accurate, task-focused signal while avoiding ego-corrupted noise.

> "Everybody has a plan until they get punched in the mouth." — Mike Tyson. The ultralearner doesn't dodge punches — they seek them out.

## Three Levels of Feedback

| Level | Signal Type | Example | Use Case |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Outcome** 🚦 | Stop/Go (pass/fail) | Sales figures, grades, public reaction | Quick assessment of overall success |
| **Informational** 🔍 | What is wrong? | Error messages, real-time results, audience confusion | Identify specific problem areas |
| **Corrective** 🛠️ | How to fix it? | Mentor pointing out technical error, solution keys | When actionable improvement steps are needed |

## The Ego Trap

Kluger & DeNisi's research warns that ego-focused feedback ("You're so smart!", "Great job!") is **corrupted data** — it masks performance deficits with noise by directing attention to self rather than task, triggering defensive or complacent postures.

Effective feedback must be **task-focused**: "Your algorithm has O(n²) complexity here — can optimize to O(n)" provides usable informational signals that accelerate learning.

## Chris Rock's Signal-Seeking Method

Comedian Chris Rock exemplifies the feedback-seeking mindset: he tests jokes anonymously at small comedy clubs, intentionally underselling delivery (slumping against a wall, reading from notes). Goal: isolate whether the joke itself is funny, stripped of celebrity persona. He seeks the "punch in the mouth from audience silence," not the empty calories of polite laughter.

## Implications for Learning

- Seek environments where feedback is rapid, accurate, and uncomfortably intense
- Prefer informational and corrective feedback over outcome-only feedback
- Avoid "praise noise" — compliments without diagnostic value
- Adjust strategy in real-time based on incoming signal

## Feedback as a Manager (Coaching Delivery)

Managerial feedback adds a relational layer to the signal-seeking model. The [[coaching]] habit delivers feedback through questions — "What's on your mind?", "And what else?", "What was most useful here for you?" — so the employee owns the correction rather than receiving it as criticism. Goldsmith's behavioral rules (source: [[sources/what-got-you-here-goldsmith]]) govern the *delivery*: react neutrally, express gratitude for all ideas, replace criticism with curiosity, and apologize sincerely. Zhuo's 1:1 model (source: [[sources/the-making-of-a-manager-zhuo]]) centers each one-on-one on what helps the employee succeed rather than on the manager's agenda.
