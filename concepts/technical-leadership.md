## Summary

**Technical Leadership** is the practice of leading through influence rather than authority — the core mechanism of the Staff+ engineering path. Unlike management, which leads through direct reports, technical leadership operates through credibility, context-sharing, and multiplying the effectiveness of others.

## Leadership Without Authority

The Staff Engineer has no direct reports but must still:

- Align stakeholders on technical direction
- Drive consensus across teams with competing priorities
- Say "no" to good ideas so the team can focus on great ones
- Frame technical challenges in terms non-technical leaders care about

The superpower is **seeing the system** — not just the code — and acting where it matters most.

## The "Humaning" Skills

Reilly describes these as "flying buttresses" — structural supports that let you build higher:

| Skill | How It Enables Leadership |
| --- | --- |
| **Communication** | Articulate vision, write strategy docs, present to executives |
| **Organizational Navigation** | Understand power dynamics, team boundaries, unspoken rules |
| **Mentorship & Sponsorship** | Grow talent, create opportunities for others, avoid becoming a bottleneck |
| **Framing Problems** | Make technical challenges matter to business leaders |
| **Delegation** | Give away the work you're good at so others can grow |

## Mental Models

Three maps help navigate ambiguity:

- **Locator Map**: "Where am I in this organization right now?"
- **Topographical Map**: "What obstacles or allies lie ahead?"
- **Treasure Map**: "What impact do I want to have in 2-3 years?"

## The Ultimate Job Description

> "Make the organization successful by solving its most important problems."

This might mean writing a three-year strategy, aligning five teams on shared architecture, hauling diesel during a data center emergency, or saying "no" to good ideas so the team focuses on great ones.
