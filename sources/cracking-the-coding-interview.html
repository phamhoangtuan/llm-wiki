Summary

A 708-page definitive guide to technical interview preparation. Covers the philosophy behind coding interviews (relative evaluation, false positives/negatives), company-specific interview cultures (Amazon's Bar Raiser, Google's Hiring Committee, Facebook's Jedi/Ninja/Pirate), 189 programming questions with solutions, and essential strategies like BUD optimization, Talk Aloud, and pattern-based learning.

Core Message

Technical interviews are not knowledge tests — they assess problem-solving ability under pressure. Your goal is to give interviewers enough data to defend you to the Hiring Committee.

Key Takeaways

  1. Relative evaluation: You're compared against every candidate the interviewer has ever seen — not an absolute scale
  2. Risk-averse hiring: Companies prefer false negatives (rejecting good candidates) over false positives (hiring bad ones)
  3. Talk Aloud: Verbalize your thought process — silence gives the interviewer nothing to report
  4. BUD optimization: After finding a solution, examine Bottlenecks, Unnecessary Work, Duplicated Work
  5. Company cultures differ: Amazon (Bar Raiser), Google (Hiring Committee), Facebook (Jedi/Ninja/Pirate) each have distinct processes
  6. Patterns over memorization: Understanding problem-solving templates beats rote memorization of 500 LeetCode solutions
