## Summary

Edited by Amy Brown and Greg Wilson, this 390-page volume lets developers learn architecture through studying real-world open-source systems. The core premise: traditional architects study thousands of buildings, but most software developers only know a handful of large programs — leading to repeated mistakes instead of building on proven successes.

## Philosophy

"Good Architects Borrow" (Le Corbusier). Software design is best taught by example, not just abstraction. By documenting internal architectures of well-known open-source applications, the book creates a shared knowledge base of patterns — and anti-patterns — that saves time and reduces technical debt.

## Why It Matters

- Accelerates growth by studying expert decisions instead of reverse-engineering from scratch
- Teaches pattern recognition across domains
- Helps avoid costly mistakes by learning what didn't work for others
