# Game Theory (Bonanno)

Giacomo Bonanno's 585-page open-access textbook on non-cooperative game theory, structured in 5 parts from ordinal preferences to incomplete information.

## Part I: Games with Ordinal Payoffs

- **Strategic Form Games**: Simultaneous-move games. Key concepts: dominance, IDSDS (Iterated Deletion of Strictly Dominated Strategies), [[nash-equilibrium|Nash equilibrium]]
- **Dynamic Games**: Sequential-move games. Backward induction, information sets, subgame-perfect equilibrium (SPE)

## Part II: Games with Cardinal Payoffs

- **Expected Utility**: von Neumann-Morgenstern utility functions for ranking probabilistic outcomes (lotteries)
- **Mixed Strategies**: Randomizing choices to make opponents indifferent; mixed-strategy Nash equilibrium
- **Dynamic Games with Cardinal Payoffs**: Behavioral strategies, SPE limitations

## Part III: Knowledge and Belief

- **Common Knowledge**: Not just "everyone knows" but "everyone knows that everyone knows..." (infinite recursion)
- **Bayesian Updating**: Revising beliefs with new information
- **Common Knowledge of Rationality (CKR)**: CKR is equivalent to IDSDS survivors

## Part IV: Equilibrium Refinements

When Nash equilibrium gives too many (sometimes unreasonable) results:

- Weak Sequential Equilibrium — sequential rationality + Bayesian updating on observed paths
- Sequential Equilibrium — adds consistency requirements for off-path beliefs
- Perfect Bayesian Equilibrium (PBE) — plausibility-based approach

## Part V: Incomplete Information

- **Harsanyi Transformation**: Converts incomplete information (unknown opponent types) into imperfect information via a "Nature" move
- **Bayesian Nash Equilibrium**: Optimizing based on probabilistic beliefs about opponent types
- Applications: reputation effects, costly signaling
