## Summary

*Becoming a Data Head* teaches non-technical professionals how to think critically about data, statistics, and machine learning without needing to code. A "Data Head" is anyone — manager, executive, or analyst — who can engage meaningfully with data work.

## Four Pillars

### 1. Thinking Like a Data Head

- **Problem definition** is the most critical and overlooked step: "Why is this important? Who does it affect?"
- Adopt a **statistical lens**: variation exists in all things. Be skeptical of "cold hard facts."
- Ask: "What assumptions are buried in this analysis?"

### 2. Speaking Like a Data Head

- **Argue with the data**: probe the "data origin story" to ensure raw material isn't garbage
- **Challenge statistics**: "compared to what?" — demand context, sample sizes, null hypotheses, confidence intervals
- **Navigate jargon**: cut through "technobabble" and "Data Science Industrial Complex" hype

### 3. Understanding the Toolbox

Intuitive understanding of models without coding:

- **Unsupervised learning**: clustering, dimensionality reduction
- **Supervised learning**: regression (predict numbers), classification (predict labels)
- **Advanced**: text analytics, deep learning for unstructured data (images, audio)

### 4. Ensuring Success

- **Data personalities**: enthusiasts vs cynics — use empathy to bridge communication gaps
- **Avoid pitfalls**: data leakage (accidentally including the answer in training data), overfitting (memorizing instead of learning)
- **Continuous learning**: individual accountability for development, often off the job

## Key Takeaway

Moving from passive consumer to leader who ensures data projects solve problems that matter.
