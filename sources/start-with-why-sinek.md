## Summary

Simon Sinek's *Start With Why* argues that great leaders and organizations don't motivate through external factors — they inspire people to act out of a sense of purpose and belonging. The book's central claim is the [[golden-circle]]: inspired leaders think, act, and communicate from the *inside out* (WHY → HOW → WHAT), summarized as **"People don't buy WHAT you do; they buy WHY you do it."**

---

## Key Claims

1. **The Golden Circle** — three concentric layers: **WHAT** (every org knows), **HOW** (differentiating value proposition), **WHY** (purpose, cause, belief — nothing to do with money, which is merely a result). Most organizations communicate outside-in; inspired ones go inside-out.

2. **Biological foundation** — the circle maps to brain anatomy: the **neocortex** (WHAT) handles rational thought and language; the **limbic brain** (WHY/HOW) governs feelings, trust, loyalty, and *all decision-making* — but has no capacity for language. This is why people can't explain "gut decisions" and rationalize them with facts afterward.

3. **Manipulation vs. inspiration** — manipulations (price drops, promotions, fear, peer pressure, novelty) drive short-term transactions but not loyalty. Inspiration creates followers who act because they were inspired; **loyalty** is turning down a better product or price to stay with a brand.

4. **The Split** — an organization's greatest challenge is its own success: as it grows, the WHY dilutes while the WHAT swells. Sustaining success requires the **School Bus Test** — extracting the founding vision into the culture so it survives the leader's departure.

5. **WHY/HOW partnerships** — visionary "WHY-types" need "HOW-types" (realists) to build the infrastructure that makes vision tangible.

6. **The Law of Diffusion** — mass-market success requires first appealing to innovators and early adopters (the left of the bell curve) who believe what the leader believes, until a tipping point is reached.

7. **The Celery Test** — a decision filter: adopt only "best practices" and products consistent with your WHY, creating authenticity and scale.

8. **Discovery, not invention** — a WHY is discovered by looking backward at the founding purpose, not invented going forward.
