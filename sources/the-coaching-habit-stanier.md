## Summary

Michael Bungay Stanier's *The Coaching Habit* (185 pages) is a slim, question-based toolkit for managers: instead of giving advice and "solving" your team's problems, you stay curious and ask a small set of powerful **"What" questions** that let people find their own answers. The habit is intentionally minimal — a few questions used consistently — because behavior change sticks when it is simple.

## The New Coaching Habit Questions

1. **"What's on your mind?"** — opens the conversation to what matters most (the book's kick-start question)
2. **"And what else?"** — unlocks deeper insights without jumping to advice
3. **"What's the real challenge here for you?"** — shifts focus from surface issues to the core problem
4. **"What do you want from me?"** — clarifies expectations and avoids assumptions
5. **"What was most useful here for you?"** — encapsulates learning and reinforces value

## Core Principles

- **Stick to "What" questions** — "What" keeps the conversation open and forward-moving; "Why" can trigger defensiveness
- **Stay curious** — the stance of not knowing beats the stance of fixing
- **Let their answers guide the way** — the person owns the problem and the solution
- **Say less, ask more** — advice-giving creates dependency; questions build self-sufficiency
