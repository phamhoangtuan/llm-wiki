# DAMA-DMBOK: Data Management Body of Knowledge (2nd Edition)

**Author:** DAMA International | **Type:** Reference/Ebook | **Pages:** 628

## Summary

The DAMA-DMBOK is the definitive reference for enterprise data management and the basis for the Certified Data Management Professional (CDMP) certification. The 2nd edition (DMBOK2) accounts for developments in Data Governance, Big Data, Data Science, and Data Ethics.

## Framework Structure

### Three Visual Models

- **DAMA Wheel**: Eleven Knowledge Areas with Data Governance at the center, balancing the other ten functions
- **Environmental Factors Hexagon**: People-process-technology relationship with Goals and Principles at the center
- **Knowledge Area Context Diagrams**: Based on SIPOC (Suppliers, Inputs, Processes, Outputs, Consumers)

### Eleven Knowledge Areas

1. **Data Governance** — Oversight and decision rights over data
2. **Data Architecture** — Blueprints aligning data assets with organizational strategy
3. **Data Modeling and Design** — Discovery and communication of data requirements
4. **Data Storage and Operations** — Implementation and support of stored data
5. **Data Security** — Maintaining privacy and preventing breaches
6. **Data Integration and Interoperability** — Movement and consolidation of data
7. **Document and Content Management** — Managing unstructured data lifecycles
8. **Reference and Master Data** — Maintaining a "version of truth" for shared entities
9. **Data Warehousing and Business Intelligence** — Managing data for analysis and reporting
10. **Metadata Management** — Enabling access to definitions and data flows
11. **Data Quality** — Measuring and improving fitness of data for use

### Dependency Models

- **Aiken's Pyramid**: Foundation phases (Modeling, Storage, Security) must precede Advanced Practices (Data Science)
- **Geuens' Model**: BI and Analytics depend on Governance, Metadata, Security, and underlying systems
- **DAMA Function Framework**: Maps KAs to lifecycle stages — Plan & Design, Enable & Maintain, Use & Enhance

### Foundational Principles

Data is an asset with unique properties. Its value should be expressed in economic terms. Managing data requires Metadata, planning, cross-functional skills, and leadership commitment. Data requirements drive IT decisions — not the reverse.
