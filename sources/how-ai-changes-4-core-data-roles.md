## Summary

Madison Mae (Learn Analytics Engineering) maps how AI is reshaping the four most common data roles. Having personally worked as a [[data-engineer]] and [[analytics-engineer]], she traces how each role's required skills are shifting — not vanishing — in the age of AI. The core thesis: **AI agents build; humans provide context, direction, and foundational knowledge.** Entry-level professionals aren't replacing seniors because foundational knowledge remains the irreplaceable differentiator.

---

## Four Roles, Four AI Shifts

### 1. [[data-analyst|Data Analyst]]

- **Core skills**: SQL, Excel, BI tools (Looker, Tableau, Power BI), A/B testing
- **Super power**: Making the most of available data to answer business questions fast
- **AI shift**: Stakeholders are starting to use dashboards primarily as filtered dataset download sources for Claude. *How data is displayed* matters less than *having clean data to feed to AI*. This makes [[data-modeling]], the core skill of [[analytics-engineer|analytics engineering]], critical for analysts to learn going forward. Without upskilling, analysts face replacement by [[self-service-analytics]].

### 2. [[data-engineer|Data Engineer]]

- **Core skills**: Python, SQL, cloud tools (AWS/Azure/GCP), database management, warehouse performance
- **Super power**: Creating stable infrastructure for performant, quality analytics pipelines
- **AI shift**: AI thrives at building and applying logic to systems. Data engineers no longer write Python scripts and pipelines from scratch — AI does this. The new skill is **using AI tools to do the building** while focusing on complex problems: handling large data cost-effectively and performantly. The role shifts from *writing* pipelines to *directing* AI to write them.

### 3. [[data-scientist|Data Scientist]]

- **Core skills**: Python (NumPy, Pandas, ScikitLearn, Matplotlib), R, basic SQL
- **Super power**: Uncovering hidden patterns in data, enabling predictive business behaviors
- **AI shift**: Similar to data engineers, AI is replacing technical-heavy work. The sharpest Python skills no longer matter as much as **understanding the business well enough to tweak models** based on how things change. The ability to use AI tools to speed up development becomes the differentiating skill.

### 4. [[analytics-engineer|Analytics Engineer]]

- **Core skills**: SQL, dimensional data modeling, modern data stack (dbt, Fivetran, Snowflake), data governance, cloud warehousing
- **Super power**: Translating business requirements into scalable, reusable data models
- **AI shift**: [[data-modeling|Data modeling]] and [[data-governance|data governance]] are becoming *more* important with AI adoption — two areas where analytics engineers already thrive. AEs need to understand how **stakeholders and AI agents** will consume their models, and shift focus toward [[semantic-layer|semantic/context layers]] to support business goals.

## The Future of Data Jobs

Key takeaways:

1. **AI agents build; humans direct**. Everyone uses AI tools — if you don't, you fall behind. But agents aren't data experts — you are.
2. **Foundational knowledge still wins**. This is why entry-level professionals don't replace senior ones. Context, direction, and judgment remain human superpowers.
3. **Data analysts face the biggest shift**. They need to learn analytics engineering skills (dbt, data modeling) to provide value. Otherwise, self-service analytics will replace them.
4. **Analytics engineers are positioned best**. Their core skills (modeling, governance) are the ones AI adoption *increases* demand for.
