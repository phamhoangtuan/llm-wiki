## Summary

Sarah Drasner's manual is written for engineers moved into leadership on technical skill or seniority rather than natural inclination — a pragmatic, empathetic, and actionable toolkit for [[engineering-management]]. It frames a manager's primary job as *enabling the people around them to do their best work together*, organized across four sections that follow the lifecycle of a manager's responsibilities.

---

## Key Claims

### Part 1 — Your Team (the human element)

1. **The value of values**. Understanding a person's fundamental beliefs provides the context for empathy and trust.
2. **Trust and vulnerability**. Trust is "built in drops but lost in buckets." Managers model vulnerability first to create psychological safety where teams learn from mistakes.
3. **Inclusivity and bias**. Healthy teams require acknowledging unconscious bias and actively building an inclusive culture.

### Part 2 — Collaboration (communication is the core work)

1. **Alignment with the "why"**. Explaining the purpose behind tasks motivates engineers more effectively than orders. See [[golden-circle|the Golden Circle]].
2. **Effective meetings and feedback**. Good meetings have a Directly Responsible Individual (DRI) and clear outcomes. [[feedback]] is a partnership aimed at growth, not a top-down critique.
3. **Change management**. A leader's job is to improve the org — guiding teams through transformation while preserving cultural support.

### Part 3 — Helping Your Team Do Their Best Work

1. **Prioritization via OKRs** — clarity that prevents organizational chaos.
2. **Scoping down PRs** — breaking large abstract work into small units reduces reviewer cognitive load and makes code easier to test.
3. **Speed vs. debt** — speed of execution means bias toward action *without* accumulating massive technical debt.

### Part 4 — Your Work (self-care as prerequisite)

1. **Managing decision fatigue** — systems for high-level and day-to-day prioritization preserve mental energy for complex leadership tasks.
2. **Setting boundaries** — be interruption-driven so the team can be focus-driven, but set firm boundaries to avoid burnout.
3. **Resilience** — a "manager cabal" or coaching helps leaders process the isolating parts of the role.
